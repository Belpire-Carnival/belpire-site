      Field guide: how the salvage loop works

      The loop: hull-scrape wrecks into Recycled Material Composite (RMC), then optionally fracture and disintegrate the hull into raw construction material. Since Alpha 4.3.2 that raw material must be refined into Construction Materials before it sells. The grade you get depends on ship class: Rubble (light salvage: fastest refining, lowest yield), Pieces, then Salvage.

          How it works

          sys://ref.howto

          The maths behind every number on this site, and the best way to use each tab.

          Every figure here is a planning aid built from community data and the numbers you type in. Star Citizen changes every patch, so check a stat or a price in game before you spend on it.
          Time to kill (TTK) for ground weapons

          The Ballistics Matrix and the FPS weapon rankings use the same steps:

          1. Damage per shot = (physical damage × the armor's physical multiplier + energy damage × the armor's energy multiplier) × pellets × the body-zone multiplier. A pistol has one pellet; a shotgun fires several at once. A multiplier of 0.70 means the armor takes 30% off.
          2. Shots to kill = the target's health ÷ damage per shot, rounded up.
          3. Time = (shots − 1) × the gap between shots. The gap is 60 ÷ rounds per minute. The first shot is instant, so only the gaps between shots count.
          4. Burst weapons add their cooldown after each full burst, on top of the normal gap.
          5. Reloads add one reload time for every full magazine you use up before the kill.

          It assumes every shot lands in the zone you pick and that nothing heals the target. Burst TTK is straight cyclic fire with no burst cooldown and no reload; TTK includes both. Damage figures come from the wiki's weapon records; where the wiki has no per-shot figure for a beam weapon, the ammunition's damage is used instead and the row is marked.

          Weapon rankings: FPS and ship

          FPS rankings score every weapon the wiki has full figures for against the armor class you choose (Light, Medium or Heavy). The armor multipliers are the middle value across that class's torso pieces, so a single odd armor does not skew them. They order fastest TTK first, and show how many weapons were left out for missing figures.

          Ship rankings use time to destroy = the reference ship's health ÷ the gun's sustained damage per second. Reference health is the ship's hull plus the shields it carries. You can filter by gun size and by engagement range, and guns with no listed range are counted but flagged. Burst damage is shown beside sustained so you can see the opening hit.

          Rankings compare guns on paper under the same assumptions. They ignore aim, bullet speed, heat and power draw, so treat them as a starting shortlist.

          Salvage, mining, trade and hauling profit
          • Salvage: value = RMC SCU × RMC price + (raw construction material SCU × refinery yield %) × its price, plus components and contract pay. Net = value − refinery fee − expenses. Per hour = net ÷ (minutes ÷ 60). Per crew member = net ÷ crew.
          • Mining: each ore line is raw SCU × yield % = refined SCU, then × price. The net takes off the refinery fee and expenses.
          • Trade chain: for each leg, quantity = the smallest of your hold, what your capital can buy, and the stock available. Profit = quantity × (sell − buy). Capital carries forward, so a good first leg funds a bigger second one. Each leg is labelled with what limited it: hold, stock or capital.
          • Hauling: payout − expenses, per hour from the minutes you enter.
          • Prices are typed or come from UEX Corp community data. They are not a live feed; each tab shows how old the figures are.
          Fabricator: how quality changes a crafted item

          Each material slot has a quality from 1 to 1000. For every stat a slot affects, the game files give a low and a high multiplier. At the quality you set, the multiplier is the low value plus (high − low) × (your quality ÷ range), and it applies to the item's base stat. The "Quality for a target" box runs that backwards: type the multiplier you want and it shows the quality needed, or says the target is out of range.

          Only straight-line (linear) curves are modelled. A stat with a stepped curve is shown as "curve not modelled" rather than guessed. Confirm one craft in game before you spend rare material.

          Fleet Manager: roles, crews and group fleet
          • Ships come from Hangar Command. The manager adds a role (combat, cargo, mining...), a status (active, standby, stored), a crew list and up to six saved builds per ship. A build is the link or code the Avionics Lab gives you; Make active marks the one you fly.
          • The overview adds up the figures of every ship that has a stat sheet: cargo SCU, DPS, hull plus shield and crew seats. A ship saved by hand has no stat sheet, so it counts as a ship but adds nothing to the totals.
          • Group fleet: each player copies their fleet code and sends it to the group; paste the codes in to see everyone's ships, roles and combined totals. Codes hold ship names, roles and figures only. There is no live shared fleet yet, so re-paste a code to refresh someone's list.
          Waypoint and Inventory
          • Waypoint lists every place that has a commodity terminal in the live UEX data. Pick where you are (or paste a line with a place name in it) and it orders results as: here, same moon or planet, same system, other systems. Within a group it puts the best price first. It cannot give distances or quantum travel times yet, Hangars, landing pads, garages and vehicle services come from the game files and are matched to places by name; storage kiosks, factions and the other switched-off filters are not known yet.
          • Inventory is a list of items by place. A scan reads a storage screen and shows you the rows to check before anything is saved; Replace this place makes the place match the scan, Add to this place only adds or raises counts. Search an item to see every place that holds it.
          Getting the most from the site
          1. Sign in with Discord so your logs, fleet, blueprints, wardrobe, loadouts and objectives follow you between devices. Without it they stay in this browser only.
          2. Import your blueprints from the Item Fabricator (Plan, Blueprints) after each patch, then browse the catalog to see what you are missing and how to unlock it.
          3. Turn a craft into objectives: plan it in the Fabricator, press Make this an objective, and tick off materials as you gather them. OmniOps shows the next one.
          4. Build ships in the Avionics Lab, save the build to your hangar, and compare it against stock. Rank the guns before you buy them.
          5. Log every run in Slag & Scrape, Extraction OS, OmniRoute and GridMaster; OmniLog totals them so you can see which job earns the most per hour.
          6. Save your kits in the Dressing Room: ground loadouts for each kind of mission, and outfits you can share as a code.
          Where the data comes from, and how fresh it is
          • Ships, parts, weapons, armor, blueprints: the Star Citizen Wiki API (community data from the game files). It updates after each patch and is cached for a day or less.
          • Commodity prices and terminals: UEX Corp, a community price database.
          • Status and news: robertsspaceindustries.com.
          • Scans (screenshots read by an AI model) are always shown to you for review before anything is saved. The model can misread; correct it before you import.
          • The patch line at the top of each tab says which game version the site was last checked against.

          Read me

          OmniGlass Desktop sits in your system tray. While Star Citizen runs it reads the Game.log file the game already writes, and it reads the RSI Launcher log once to find out which install you launched. From that it shows your blueprint, contract, mission and ship-loss events, your blueprints from the game files, minerals, reputation ladders, and an optional in-game overlay.

          It never reads game memory, injects code, or sends keystrokes or mouse input. To open its windows inside the game it listens for its own keys only (F3, F5 to F8) while Star Citizen has the keyboard, and always passes them on to the game. It reads your Game.log, your archived logs and the game's own data file (Data.p4k) read-only, and draws its own windows.

          The one optional feature that writes is Field Notes. It is off until you switch it on in Settings. When you press Apply it writes the game's English text file (global.ini, built from your own install) and makes sure user.cfg selects English, after saving your originals. Roll back restores them exactly. It refuses to write while the game is running. CIG allows community text files but does not endorse them, so it is your choice.

          What it sends here (only while the PC is linked and sending is on): blueprints you receive, contracts, missions and your own ship losses. Never other players' names or ids.

          Game data (blueprints, ores, reputation) comes from the site, one file set per channel (LIVE and PTU), extracted from the game files by us. Check for updates after each patch.

          What is in the window

          • Events: what the log shows, with a filter and a copy-as-text button that leaves your handle out.
          • Blueprints: all blueprints for the build, which contracts give them (CAN TAKE, LOCKED or SET RANK), and which you own. Export and import as JSON or CSV.
          • Minerals: search an ore, see its compositions and where it spawns.
          • Reputation: pick your rank on each ladder so contracts show whether you can take them.
          • Settings: checks that the app can read your game files, imports game data, reads your older logs for blueprints you already own, and holds Field Notes: blueprint marks and counts on contract titles, and rank, reputation, full blueprint lists and weapon numbers in descriptions.
          • Overlay: a click-through window over the game made of cards you can move and resize: Recent events, your Watching list, a This session count, Timers and pop-up Alerts. See the hotkey guide below.
          • Website connection: with one switch and a short code, this site can show your live game data (see the how-to below).
          • Watch log of: pick LIVE, PTU or any other install the launcher knows.

          How to: first run

          1. Start the app. Its window opens and an icon appears in the tray (left-click it to bring the window back).
          2. Check the Game.log line shows your install, for example the LIVE folder. If not, press Use this file after typing the path.
          3. Under Game data pick LIVE or PTU and press Check for updates once.
          4. To send your events here: open OmniOps, find the OmniGlass Desktop card, choose Link a device, and type the code it shows into the app, then press Link. Untick Send to my profile to pause sending; Unlink this PC removes the link.
          5. Play. Blueprints you receive appear in the app and on your Blueprints page within a few minutes.

          How to: live sync from scmdb optional

          1. In the app scroll to the bottom, open scmdb live sync, tick Listen for scmdb and press Copy address.
          2. On scmdb.net open Profile, then Sync Sinks, add a sink, paste the address, tick Blueprints and save.
          3. Press Resync on that sink. The app's counter shows how many messages arrived and how many new blueprints were added.

          From then on, each blueprint you mark as owned in scmdb is added to the app while it is running; anything marked while it was closed arrives on the next Resync. Blueprints are only ever added, never removed.

          The address works only on your own PC (127.0.0.1) and contains a secret: treat it like a password. Make a new secret replaces it, then paste the new address into scmdb.

          Hotkey guide

          • F3 or F5: open the OmniGlass window over the game. Press again to hide it; the keyboard goes back to the game.
          • F6: show or hide the overlay cards (Recent, Watching, This session, Timers).
          • F7: move and resize the overlay cards. Esc, Done or F7 again saves and finishes.
          • F8: search a blueprint or mineral. Type, press Enter to pin the top result to Watching, Esc (or F8 again) to close. The keyboard goes back to the game.
          • These work in menus and in the world, while the app is running. Settings shows the last key the app received and how it arrived. The overlay only shows over the game in borderless windowed mode.
          • If Star Citizen or the RSI Launcher runs as administrator, run OmniGlass as administrator too, or Windows keeps the keys from reaching it.

          How to: set up the in-game overlay

          1. Run Star Citizen in borderless windowed mode, then press F6.
          2. Press F7. The screen dims and a bar appears. Drag a card to move it; drag its bottom-right corner to resize it. Tick or untick Recent, Watching, Alerts, Session and Timers to show or hide each card.
          3. There are two saved layouts, Flying and On foot. Choose one in the bar before you arrange; you switch between them by hand. Reset layout puts the current one back to the default.
          4. Press Done (or Esc). Positions are kept on this PC as a share of your screen, so they survive a change of resolution.

          Dragging only moves the app's own window. Nothing is sent to the game.

          How to: timers and the session card

          1. In the app open Settings, then In-game timers. Type a label such as Refinery Pyro and the time the game shows: 2h 10m, 45m or 1:30:00 (up to 7 days, eight timers at once). Press Start.
          2. The Timers card counts down on the overlay and posts a Timer done alert when it ends. Timers survive restarting the app.

          The game does not write refinery jobs to its log, so these timers run on the time you type; they do not read the game.

          The This session card counts what the log shows since the game started: contracts accepted, missions completed, blueprints received, ships destroyed and lost, and times you went down. The clock stops when the game closes.

          How to: show your live game data here

          1. In the app open Settings and tick Let the website connect. The app shows an 8-character code and copies it for you.
          2. On this site open OmniOps, find the From your game card under the OmniGlass Desktop card, type the code and press Connect.
          3. Your recent events and game data build then show live. Use Disconnect at the bottom of that card to stop.

          The connection stays on your own PC (127.0.0.1) and only accepts this site. New code in the app cuts off anything already connected. Untick the switch to turn it off.

          How to: more

          • Back up or restore my settings (bottom of the window) saves your owned blueprints, watch list, ranks and window settings as one file. Your profile link and scmdb secret are never in it.
          • Read again re-reads the whole Game.log from the top.
          • Moving to a new PC: install, restore the backup, link again with a new code.
          • Field Notes names (Settings, Field Notes): rename the label on added lines and the two blueprint tags. Press Preview or Apply to put a new name into the game.

          If something looks wrong

          • Stuck on "Starting..." or empty fields: quit the app from the tray and start it again. Report it with the first red line from the console if it repeats.
          • A key says "taken by another program" in Settings: another program, or an older copy of the app, holds it. Quit the old copy; the rest of the app still works.
          • No overlay over the game: switch the game to borderless windowed mode. Check Game.log shows your install.
          • A card is off screen or in the wrong place after changing monitors: press F7, then Reset layout.
          • Data for another build: the data bar warns when the game data is for a different patch than your install. Press Check for updates.
          • Nothing arrives from scmdb: make sure the app is running with the box ticked, then press Resync in scmdb.

A new scan replaces the categories it shows, so blueprints you no longer see there drop off.

          Data: only what you scan or type. There is no game API for blueprints, so re-scan before you play; OmniOps' checklist shows how old your last import is.

          Armory

          Avionics Lab builds a loadout: pick a ship, click any part to see every figure the wiki has for it, swap it from a sortable table (green is better than what is fitted, red is worse), and see damage, defence, power and flight totals against stock. Under the builder, Ship weapon rankings orders ship guns by time to destroy a reference ship. Ballistics Matrix holds the FPS time-to-kill calculator and FPS weapon rankings, plus medpen tracking. The Dressing Room holds your Wardrobe (browse gear for each slot, dye it, save outfits to a file or share them as a code) and your Ground Loadouts.

          Data: Star Citizen Wiki item database through our own snapshot. It is only as current as the wiki and the last snapshot rebuild.

          Earn

          Slag & Scrape is hull scraping, structural salvage and refining. Extraction OS plans a refinery job, prices the ore and logs the haul. OmniRoute lists live buy and sell pairs and plans a route. GridMaster packs 1 to 32 SCU boxes into your hold and gives a loading order.

          Data: UEX commodity prices. A chip marked LIVE is community-submitted and can be hours old or wrong; SNAPSHOT is a dated reference.

          Log

          OmniLog is every run you logged, totalled and sorted by activity. Logging a run from any Earn tab feeds the OmniOps totals and best rate.

          Data: only what you logged. Nothing here comes from the game.

          OmniGlass Desktop Windows app

          A small tray app that reads your Game.log and sends only your blueprints, contracts, missions and ship losses to your profile. The OmniGlass Desktop tab has the read me, the how-to (including the optional scmdb live sync) and the download with its checksum.

          Reading the weapon rankings

          • TTK is the time from first shot to kill, assuming every shot lands in the chosen zone and the first shot fires at time zero. Bullet drop, damage falloff and range are not modelled for FPS weapons.
          • Burst TTK is straight cyclic fire with no burst cooldown and no reload. TTK adds burst cooldowns and the reload time you set. The game data has no reload time, so one value applies to every weapon (0 ignores reloads).
          • Shotguns look unbeatable because every pellet is assumed to hit. Treat them as close-range figures.
          • Ship guns are scored against a reference ship: its hull plus the shields it carries from the factory, divided by the gun's damage per second. Armor, shield absorption, hit location and gun convergence are not modelled.
          • Damage type is Physical, Energy or Mixed from the per-hit figures; ship guns can also be Distortion. Guns with no range in the data drop out when you set a minimum range, and the table says how many.

          Glossary

          aUEC
          The in-game currency, alpha United Earth Credits.
          SCU
          Standard cargo unit; boxes come in 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 SCU.
          TTK
          Time to kill.
          DPS
          Damage per second. Sustained counts cooldowns or heat over a longer window; burst is the peak.
          Hardpoint
          A slot on a ship that holds a weapon, turret, missile rack or other component.
          Ordnance
          Missiles and torpedoes, held in racks that are locked to a size.
          Hull / shield
          Hull is the ship's structure; shield is the regenerating layer in front of it.
          RMC
          Recycled material composite, what hull scraping produces.
          LIVE / SNAPSHOT
          LIVE figures were fetched just now; SNAPSHOT figures are a dated reference.
